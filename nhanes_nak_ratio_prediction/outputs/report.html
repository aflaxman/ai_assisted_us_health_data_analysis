NHANES dietary Na/K prediction — proof of concept

Question. Can demographic and clinical predictors that are common to both NHANES and EHR data be used to estimate a population's mean dietary Na/K ratio at sub-national geographic scales?

Design. Train (NHANES 2007-2016, n = 26,047 adults with reliable 24-hour dietary recall and full features) → Holdout (NHANES 2017-2018, n = 4,674). Best model: LightGBM.

Result. Individual-level holdout R² = 0.097, RMSE = 0.482, MAE = 0.377. Overall holdout-population mean Na/K = 1.394 observed vs 1.367 predicted (bias -0.027 mg/mg, ≈ -1.9%). At county-scale draws (N=1000) the 95% prediction interval for the population mean is ±~0.03 Na/K units (≈ ±2% of the mean).

1 · Na/K distribution: train vs holdout (observed) and model predictions

The predicted distribution is much narrower than the observed distribution because demographics + clinical predictors capture mean differences, not within-stratum intake variability. Mean prediction at a population scale is the operationally relevant endpoint.

4 · Catchment-mean error vs sample size

500 random clusters per N drawn (with replacement) from the holdout. Cluster mean prediction error converges toward the small population-level bias as catchment size grows. With N≈1000 (a small-county scale) the 95% interval is well under ±0.05 Na/K units (≈4% of the overall mean).

5 · Feature importance (LightGBM)

% of total gain. Age, race/ethnicity, and adiposity (waist, BMI) dominate; clinical biomarkers (SBP/DBP, HTN/DM diagnosis) contribute modestly. Predictors marked here are all available in standard EHR demographics+vitals+problem-list extracts.

6 · Secular trend across NHANES cycles

Survey-weighted mean Na/K (±95% CI) by cycle. The dashed line shows the LightGBM predicted mean for the 2017-2018 holdout. Modest secular drift in the underlying diet explains part of the small overall bias.
